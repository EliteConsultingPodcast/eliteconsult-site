What is your situation?

I have a qualification and I speak German

If you have a qualification in medicine, nursing, care of older people, a medical or dental practice, early childhood education, teaching, psychology or social work, and you speak German (usually B1–B2; for medicine, C1), you can start work. We work with hundreds of first-class employers across Germany and look with you for the post that best fits your experience and your wishes. We also prepare doctors to work in Germany: recognition of the qualification and the medical German exam (Fachsprachprüfung).

I have a qualification, but I don't speak German (or I am still studying)

Your qualification is worth a great deal in Germany; the language is all you lack. We organise your German course here in Las Palmas or online. At the same time we prepare the recognition of your qualification, even while you are still studying, so that when you finish you can start without losing time.

I speak German, but I have no qualification in the sector

You can do an Ausbildung directly in Germany: paid dual training of 2 to 3 years in nursing, care of older people, early childhood education, a medical practice or hospitality. You are paid from the first month, about €1,000–1,650 gross a month depending on the profession, and you finish with a recognised qualification.

I have no qualification and I don't speak German

There is a path for you too. You can take suitable training in Spain and learn German alongside it, or start as an au pair for 6–12 months with a German family (accommodation, meals and pocket money) and then do your Ausbildung. We guide you so you can choose the path that suits you best.

Why Germany

A country that needs qualified people

Germany has a long-running shortage of qualified staff (Fachkräftemangel), especially in health, care and education. The population is ageing, and the need for trained professionals remains.

For someone who arrives well prepared, the country offers stable contracts, social security, a sound balance between work and private life, and a path towards permanent residence. Orientation for that step is free and without obligation.

Medicine and nursing

Health care and care of older people

We support doctors, nurses, nursing assistants and people working in care of older people (Altenpflege).

Doctors

To practise as a doctor in Germany, German at C1 is what is normally required, and recognition of the qualification is necessary.

Indicative salary: €80,000–150,000+ gross a year.

Nurses

Nursing (Pflegefachkraft) is a regulated profession: recognition is required in order to practise. The German level usually asked for is B2.

Spanish qualifications that can lead to recognition — each case is looked at individually:

  • Grado en Enfermería (bachelor’s degree in nursing)
  • Diplomado/a en Enfermería (former nursing diploma)
  • ATS (Ayudante Técnico Sanitario, the older nursing title)

Indicative salary: €44,000–60,000 gross a year. It can be higher with experience and shift work.

Assistants and care of older people

In care of older people (Altenpflege) and in assistant posts, these Spanish qualifications are the ones we see most often:

  • TCAE (Técnico/a en Cuidados Auxiliares de Enfermería — nursing assistant)
  • TAPSD (Técnico/a en Atención a Personas en Situación de Dependencia — care of dependent people)
  • Técnico en Atención Sociosanitaria (the older title in social and health care)
  • Vocational certificates in social and health care (residential settings and home care)

Some posts accept B1; others ask for B2. Requirements depend on the post and the employer.

What employers usually offer

Depending on the employer and the post, offers in this field often include:

  • €4,000–6,000 gross a month, depending on experience, post and supplements
  • A permanent contract
  • Supplements for nights, weekends and public holidays
  • 30 days of holiday
  • Full-time or part-time hours
  • German courses and further training
  • Help with housing
  • International teams

A nurse and an assistant are not the same

A nurse (Pflegefachfrau, Pflegefachmann or Pflegefachperson) holds the regulated nursing profession: a full qualification, and recognition in order to practise. An assistant or care worker supports day-to-day care. The qualification, the German level and whether recognition is needed depend on that specific post. That is why we look at your title with you before suggesting a path.

No qualification in the sector

Paid training in Germany (Ausbildung)

With an Ausbildung (dual training of 2 to 3 years) you learn a profession, earn a salary from the first month and finish with a recognised qualification. As an EU citizen you do not need a visa or a work permit. What matters most is German, usually B1–B2, and we help you organise it.

Nursing and care of older people (Pflegefachfrau/-mann)

3 years. Pay during training is about €1,490–1,650 gross a month; once you qualify, about €3,500. Demand is very high, and the qualification is valid throughout the EU.

Nursing and geriatric assistant (Pflegehelfer/in)

Usually 1 year. A quick way into the sector and a bridge to nursing.

Medical or dental practice assistant (MFA / ZFA)

3 years, usually with German at B2. Pay is about €1,000–1,250 a month during training.

Early childhood education (Erzieher/in, Kinderpfleger/in)

Work with children in nurseries and schools. In some regions the training is paid.

Hospitality and gastronomy

Hotel reception, restaurant or kitchen, over 2 to 3 years, with German at B1. Pay is about €1,100–1,340 a month.

Au pair as a first step

6 to 12 months with a family, with accommodation, meals and pocket money. Ideal for learning German before you start an Ausbildung.

Indicative figures as of November 2026. Monthly gross amounts under the collective agreement; they can vary by region and employer.

Common questions

Before you write to us

Do I need to have my qualification recognised?

For regulated professions, yes. Medicine and nursing (Pflegefachkraft) are regulated: without recognition you cannot practise. For other profiles it depends on the post and, in education, also on the region. We check it with you.

What level of German do I need?

As a guide: B1 to start in most sectors, B2 in health and care, and C1 for doctors. In some care or education posts B1 can be enough; B2 is recommended. The exact level depends on the profession and the employer.

My qualification has a different name. Does it count?

Do not rule it out. Many Spanish qualifications are not called the same thing in Germany. Send us your details and we will look at the case.

How much does orientation cost?

Orientation is free, and there is no obligation.

Can I go to Germany with no qualification and no German?

Yes. There are paths: training in Spain with German alongside it, au pair, or a paid Ausbildung in Germany. We guide you free of charge so you can choose yours.

Salaries are indicative and gross; they vary with experience, region and employer.
